# Discovery and Recovery

Discovery and recovery manage uncertain history and controlled restoration within [VP's full project quality management method](how-vp-works.md). The owner mandate, retained evidence and reviewed changes govern both.

Some projects contain useful work but lack a reliable account of what exists, what succeeded, and why decisions were made. Discovery maps that territory. Recovery changes it.

## Discovery Is Read-Only

Discovery reconstructs:

- the terminal goal;
- the locations and provenance of artefacts;
- which outcomes are valuable, failed, partial, or unknown;
- which claims have supporting evidence;
- what is still missing.

The user's memory is primary evidence for intent and historical value. It is not proof of technical behaviour. Files, history, tests, and reproduced results provide that evidence.

The discovery report must stand alone because the conversation that produced it may later be unavailable. Full confirmed restatements are therefore more valuable than compressed summaries.

See [Project Discovery](../playbooks/discovery.md) for the operational process.

## Verification Is the Boundary

Before recovery, verify the discovery report against the conversation and source material. Check that exclusions are explicit, conclusions are supported, user-confirmed facts are distinguished from inference, and unresolved questions remain visible.

This checkpoint prevents context loss from silently becoming project history. See [Discovery Verification](../playbooks/discovery-verification.md).

## Recovery Is Controlled Mutation

Recovery turns the verified map into a sequence of changes. Each step should have:

- one clear action;
- a reason tied to discovery evidence;
- preconditions;
- a verification method;
- a reversal path;
- a recorded result.

Preserve or archive before deleting. Stop when observations diverge materially from the plan. A tidy directory is not evidence of a successful recovery; valuable outcomes must remain accessible and reproducible.

See [Project Recovery](../playbooks/recovery.md) for the operational process.

## Why the Boundary Matters

Changing files while their value is still uncertain destroys evidence. Conversely, continuing discovery after the project is understood can become avoidance. The verified discovery report is the boundary: before it, learn; after it, change deliberately.
